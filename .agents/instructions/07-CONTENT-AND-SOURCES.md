# Content authority, provenance, and publishing

## Sources and verification

Start with the current official [NCOGA site](https://ncoga.com/) and review its relevant About, mission, history, leadership, membership, community, news, events, partnerships, contact, and YAY pages. Treat that site as an initial source, not as an error-free migration set: its About page has contained unrelated template material. Read the actual [September 2026 YAY pages update](../../references/PAGES%20UPDATE%20SEPTEMBER%202026.md) and [YAY website review meeting notes](../../references/Website%20updates%20meeting%20%28Tiz%202026%29.md). Both are source documents, not files to rewrite during content work. Verify current links, dates, titles, and publication authorization before use.

Content authority order: explicit owner instruction; approved dated organizational update; current verified official content; older official content; proposed editorial copy. A newer draft is not automatically an approved statement for every fact. Never invent mission wording, leadership roles or biographies, milestones, events, testimonials, impact statistics, partners, or contact details. Keep official mission and vision distinct from proposed headlines.

Classify records as **verified**, **approved update**, **proposed**, **pending review**, or **obsolete**. Record title, destination route, source URL/document and date, last verification, owner, status, notes, missing materials, duplication, and publication status in [content inventory](../../docs/content/NCOGA_CONTENT_INVENTORY.md). Retain original publication dates and attribution during migration; do not backdate or make old announcements appear current. Exclude demonstrable theme/demo content.

## Editorial and media rules

Use clear, specific human language and identify agent-proposed copy as such until approved. Keep actual NCOGA and YAY activity distinct from illustrative material. Prefer permissioned official photography, then licensed documentary or archival material with attribution; identify stock honestly. Never generate fictional people and imply they are NCOGA members or leaders.

Verify external registration, partnership, and social links. Store dates with timezones. News needs accurate authorship, dates, organization ownership, draft/review/scheduled/published/archive state, metadata, and suitable imagery. Events need owner, visibility, time/location, registration state, and lifecycle; past events move to archives. Shared article and event models may serve NCOGA and YAY with explicit ownership.

Before choosing a CMS, inspect existing publishing infrastructure and investigate retaining WordPress. Compare cost, editorial workflow, auth, hosting, maintenance, images, drafts, scheduling, roles, preview, security, and migration. Do not introduce a CMS merely because one is named in the master prompt. See [information architecture](08-INFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE.md).
